# RJ-Books Research & Storyboard

## Stephen's New Friends

---

## Psychological Research Summary

### Color Psychology for Children (Ages 3-7)
**Core Palette for RJ-Books:**
- **Warm Yellow (#F4D03F):** Optimism, energy, friendship — perfect for hope/joy moments
- **Soft Blue (#85C1E9):** Trust, calm, safety — use during sad/anxious moments
- **Sage Green (#82E0AA):** Growth, new beginnings, nature — transitions and hope
- **Gentle Coral (#F1948A):** Warmth, comfort, family bonds — consoling scenes
- **Sky Blue (#AED6F1):** Openness, possibility — new beginnings
- **Sunset Orange (#F8C471):** Creativity, excitement — construction/building joy

**Avoid:**
- Harsh reds (anger, danger — unless intentional)
- Dark purples (mystery/confusion for this age)
- Over-saturated neons (overwhelming for sensitive children)

### Visual Storytelling for Neurodivergent Children
- **Clear focal points:** One main subject per illustration
- **Consistent character design:** Same outfit/colors throughout for recognition
- **Gentle transitions:** Soft color shifts between emotional scenes
- **Predictable patterns:** Rhythm in page layouts (sad→hope pattern)
- **White space:** Don't overcrowd — overwhelmed kids need visual breathing room

### Animal Archetype Research
- **German Shepherd with Rottweiler coloring:** Loyal protector, gentle giant — perfect comfort companion
- **Dogs in children's literature:** Unconditional love, emotional support, bridge between child and world
- **Jackson's role:** Physical comfort (leaning, licking), non-judgmental presence, routine anchor

### Dyslexia-Friendly Design
- **Sans-serif fonts:** OpenDyslexic or similar
- **Generous line spacing:** 1.5x minimum
- **Short sentences:** 5-8 words per line for early readers
- **High contrast:** Dark text on light background
- **Left-aligned text:** Easier tracking for struggling readers
- **Ragged right edge:** More natural than justified

## Storyboard: 14 Spreads (28 pages)

### Spread 1: Title Page
**Visual:** Stephen and Jackson sitting on their porch, waving at the house across the street. Warm afternoon light.
**Colors:** Full warm palette — established happy world
**Text:** "Stephen's New Friends — An RJ-Books Story"

### Spread 2: "Meet Stephen"
**Visual:** Stephen in his white house, playing with Jackson. German Shepherd with Rottweiler markings (black and tan). White house with porch.
**Colors:** Bright, happy — yellows, blues, greens
**Text:** "This is Stephen. He is four years old. Stephen lives in a white house with Mommy, Daddy, and Jackson."
**Psychology:** Establishes safety, routine, belonging. Jackson is clearly visible as companion.

### Spread 3: "The Friends Across the Street"
**Visual:** Stephen at window, waving to neighbors. Their house visible — warm, lived-in, flowers in yard.
**Colors:** Warm corals and yellows — connection, love
**Text:** "Stephen loves the people who live across the street. They wave every morning. They smile every night."
**Psychology:** Establishes attachment before loss. Kids need to see WHAT is being lost to feel the emotion.

### Spread 4: "One Day..."
**Visual:** Stephen at window, confused. Empty house across street. No car. Darker sky hint.
**Colors:** Shift to muted blues — subtle worry
**Text:** "One day, Stephen does not see them. Their car is gone. Their lights are dark."
**Psychology:** First sign of change. Notice — don't tell. Show confusion through posture.

### Spread 5: "The Bad Day"
**Visual:** Excavators tearing down the house. Stephen watching from window, tears. Jackson beside him, head lowered.
**Colors:** Blues and grays — sadness. BUT: Stephen's pajamas stay bright (anchor of stability)
**Text:** "The next day, big machines come. CRASH! BANG! The house across the street comes down. Stephen cries."
**Psychology:** Acknowledge grief. Machines = chaos/loss. Jackson's presence = "you're not alone."

### Spread 6: "Jackson Knows"
**Visual:** Close-up of Jackson comforting Stephen. Licking tears. Stephen hugging Jackson's neck.
**Colors:** Soft corals returning — comfort, warmth emerging from sadness
**Text:** "Jackson knows when Stephen is sad. Jackson leans close. Jackson licks the tears away. 'I love you,' says Jackson."
**Psychology:** Animal as emotional co-regulator. Physical comfort before words. Love without explanation.

### Spread 7: "Mommy and Daddy Help"
**Visual:** Parents sitting with Stephen, all on floor, Jackson included. Group hug. Stephen still sad but not alone.
**Colors:** Warm family tones — coral, soft yellow, sage green
**Text:** "Mommy sits with Stephen. Daddy holds his hand. 'It is okay to be sad,' they say. 'We are here.'"
**Psychology:** Validates emotions. Adults don't fix — they witness. "It's okay to be sad" = permission to feel.

### Spread 8: "A Few Days Later"
**Visual:** Cement mixer pouring foundation. Stephen watching from porch, curious but cautious. New hole in ground = new possibility.
**Colors:** Blues shifting to greens — transition from sad to curious
**Text:** "A few days later, a cement mixer comes. VROOM! It pours a new floor. 'What is happening?' asks Stephen."
**Psychology:** Concrete = foundation = new beginning. Question shows engagement returning.

### Spread 9: "Daddy's Promise"
**Visual:** Daddy pointing to foundation, Stephen looking up, hopeful. Blueprint or imaginary house in clouds.
**Colors:** Sky blues, sage greens — hope, growth
**Text:** "Daddy smiles. 'A new house is coming,' he says. 'And do you know what? New friends will live there!'"
**Psychology:** Reframe loss as transformation. Not "replacement" — new chapter. Father = wisdom, forward-looking.

### Spread 10: "Every Morning"
**Visual:** Stephen in pajamas, sitting outside daily. Time-lapse feel: different weather, same spot. Construction progressing.
**Colors:** Building warmth — yellows increasing each day
**Text:** "Every morning, Stephen sits outside. He watches the walls go up. He watches the roof grow. He waves to the workers."
**Psychology:** Routine = healing. Engagement with process = empowerment. Workers = friendly adults (safety).

### Spread 11: "Breakfast with Jackson"
**Visual:** Stephen and Jackson eating breakfast on porch. Beams going up for roof in background. Joy moment.
**Colors:** Full bright palette — yellow, green, blue sky
**Text:** "One special day, Stephen and Jackson eat breakfast outside. UP, UP, UP go the beams for the new roof!"
**Psychology:** Shared joy with companion. "Special day" = ordinary magic. Building = creation = life going on.

### Spread 12: "New Trees"
**Visual:** Workers planting trees and flowers. Stephen helping (small shovel). Green everywhere.
**Colors:** Dominated by sage greens — growth, nature, life
**Text:** "The workers plant trees. They plant flowers. Stephen helps. 'This will be a happy home,' says Stephen."
**Psychology:** Participation = agency. Trees = slow growth = patience. Stephen's words show ownership of new narrative.

### Spread 13: "Where Are They?"
**Visual:** Empty construction site. No workers. Stephen alone, worried. Jackson beside him, alert.
**Colors:** Subdued — back to blues briefly. But trees remain green (anchor of hope)
**Text:** "One day, the workers do not come. Stephen waits. Stephen worries. 'Where is everyone?'"
**Psychology:** Brief setback = emotional realism. Life isn't linear. Worry before joy makes joy earned.

### Spread 14: "The Moving Van"
**Visual:** Big moving truck arrives. Boxes. Stephen watching, excited but nervous. Jackson standing tall.
**Colors:** Building excitement — warm tones returning
**Text:** "A few days later, a BIG truck comes. Boxes come out. A new family is here!"
**Psychology:** Transition moment. Moving van = change = unknown. Stephen's feelings mixed (like real kids).

### Spread 15: "Meet Nora"
**Visual:** New family unloading. Girl Stephen's age (Nora) holding a stuffed animal. Stephen hiding slightly behind Jackson.
**Colors:** Soft focus on Nora — new warmth. Stephen still in blues (nervous)
**Text:** "A girl comes out. She is Stephen's age. Her name is Nora. She has a teddy bear."
**Psychology:** Peer introduction. Similar age = potential friend. Teddy bear = shared childness. Stephen behind Jackson = safety seeking.

### Spread 16: "Nervous Stephen"
**Visual:** Stephen with family, walking across street. Holding Daddy's hand. Jackson leading the way. Stephen's face = worried but brave.
**Colors:** Mix of nervous blue and courageous coral
**Text:** "Stephen is nervous. His tummy feels funny. But Daddy holds his hand. Jackson walks first."
**Psychology:** Names the feeling (anxiety). Support system visible. Dog as brave leader. "Funny tummy" = kid language for anxiety.

### Spread 17: "Saying Hello"
**Visual:** Stephen and Nora face to face. Stephen offering a flower (from his yard). Nora smiling. Parents watching proudly.
**Colors:** Full warm palette — breakthrough moment
**Text:** "'Hello,' says Stephen. He gives Nora a flower. 'Hello,' says Nora. She gives him a smile."
**Psychology:** Simple exchange. Flower = peace offering, gentleness. Smile = reciprocation. Mutual vulnerability.

### Spread 18: "The End... or the Beginning?"
**Visual:** Stephen and Nora playing together. Jackson watching. Both houses visible — Stephen's white house, Nora's new house.
**Colors:** Bright, balanced, complete
**Text:** "Stephen and Nora are friends. They play every day. Sometimes change is scary. But sometimes... it brings new friends."
**Psychology:** Resolution with wisdom. "End or beginning" = life cycles. Direct message: change can be good.

### Spread 19: Back Cover
**Visual:** RJ-Books logo, Stephen and Nora holding hands, Jackson between them.
**Colors:** Brand palette
**Text:** "RJ-Books — Stories for big feelings, little hearts."

## Character Design Notes

### Stephen
- **Age:** 4 years old
- **Hair:** Brown, slightly messy
- **Features:** Expressive eyes (show worry, joy, curiosity)
- **Clothes:** Bright pajamas (consistent — becomes visual anchor). Suggest: blue and yellow stripes
- **Posture:** Starts small/nervous, grows more confident through story
- **Key trait:** Always has one hand on Jackson when uncertain

### Jackson
- **Breed:** German Shepherd body, Rottweiler coloring (black and tan)
- **Expression:** Wise, gentle, patient
- **Body language:** Leans into Stephen when sad, stands tall when Stephen needs courage
- **Detail:** Tongue out when happy, head tilted when concerned

### Nora
- **Age:** 4 years old
- **Hair:** Different from Stephen (suggest: curly red hair for visual contrast)
- **Feature:** Carries small teddy bear (shows she's also finding comfort in transition)
- **Clothes:** Simple dress, different color from Stephen (suggest: soft green)

### Parents
- **Style:** Warm, approachable, present but not overwhelming
- **Mommy:** Gentle features, often kneeling to Stephen's level
- **Daddy:** Slightly taller perspective, points and explains
- **Consistency:** Same clothes throughout (visual anchors)

### Construction Workers
- **Diversity:** Mixed genders, ethnicities
- **Expression:** Friendly, waving, inclusive
- **Safety:** Hard hats, vests (teachable moment about safety)

## Typography Recommendations

**Primary Font:** OpenDyslexic or similar sans-serif
**Size:** Large (18-20pt for picture books)
**Line spacing:** 1.5x
**Text placement:** Bottom or side of illustration, never over busy backgrounds
**Contrast:** Dark charcoal text on light cream background (easier than pure black/white)

---

## Layout Notes

**Page dimensions:** Standard picture book 8.5" x 8.5" or 8" x 10"
**Bleed:** 0.125" all sides for print
**Left pages:** Often Stephen's internal world (feelings)
**Right pages:** External events (action, resolution)
**Full spreads:** Major emotional moments (Spreads 5, 11, 18)

## Emotional Journey Map

```
Spread 1-3: Safety, attachment
Spread 4-5: Loss, confusion, grief
Spread 6-7: Comfort, co-regulation, permission to feel
Spread 8-12: Curiosity, hope, engagement, joy
Spread 13: Brief worry (emotional realism)
Spread 14-16: Anticipation, anxiety, courage
Spread 17-18: Connection, friendship, wisdom
```

**Color temperature arc:**
Warm → Cool (sad) → Warm (comfort) → Building warmth → Brief cool → Warm resolution
